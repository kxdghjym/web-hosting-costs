## What web hosting services actually include

A web hosting service rents you space on a server that stays online around the clock and delivers your site to every visitor. Storage, bandwidth, email, SSL certificates, and backups get packaged differently depending on which of the four main types you buy, and those four cover almost everything on the market.

**Shared hosting** puts your site on a server alongside other sites, which is what makes it the cheapest tier. GoDaddy's Economy plan is $5.99/mo for the first three-year term with 25 GB of NVMe storage, cPanel, a free domain, free email, and a free SSL certificate for the first year; Deluxe and Ultimate step up to 10 sites/50 GB ($7.99) and 25 sites/75 GB ($12.99) on the same term structure. Hostinger's shared plans span $2.99–$70/mo depending on resources, with 20–100 GB of storage and a free domain for the first year.

**Managed WordPress** is hosting tuned specifically for WordPress with the maintenance handled for you. GoDaddy's WordPress Basic plan ($5.99 for the first three-year term, 10 GB NVMe) ships with WordPress pre-installed, weekly backups, automatic core updates, and an SSL certificate with no renewal fees. Kinsta sits at the premium end at $35/mo, tagged by Forbes Advisor as the best managed WordPress host, with the first month free on its entry plan.

**VPS** gives you a guaranteed slice of a server with root access. GoDaddy sells VPS plans as self-managed or fully managed, with daily backups and on-demand snapshots, plus Windows VPS with Plesk Obsidian for ASP.NET apps. For a mainstream price reference, InMotion's current VPS promotion is $17.99/mo, a 68% discount on a one-year 8 vCPU plan.

**Cloud and dedicated** round out the list. Cloudways offers a 3-day free trial with no credit card required, TechRadar recommends ScalaHosting's cloud infrastructure for business-critical stores because of its scalability and redundancy, and PCMag rates AccuWeb 4.0 "Excellent" as its dedicated-hosting pick, with Linux and Windows available at every tier.

## Which type of hosting fits your site

The decision comes down to three things: how much traffic you expect, how much server administration you want to do, and what an hour of downtime costs you. Mapped to real situations:

1. **First blog or portfolio.** The cheapest credible shared tier is enough. GoDaddy's Economy plan covers one site on 25 GB NVMe with daily backups, unmetered bandwidth, and a 30-day money-back guarantee. Your main watch-out is resource caps, not features.
2. **Media-heavy sites.** Look at inode limits — the number of files and folders your account allows, where every image and PDF counts. HostingStep measured Hostinger's allowance at 400,000 to 2,000,000 depending on plan, against an industry norm around 250,000. Upload-heavy sites hit that ceiling before they hit any bandwidth cap, and almost no roundup mentions it.
3. **Sites with regular traffic spikes.** In HostingStep's most recent twelve-month window (November 2025 through August 2026), Bluehost posted 99.99% uptime and absorbed sudden spikes without issue — strong enough that HostingStep recommended it for the first time in six years of testing. Hostinger's Unlimited plan ($3.69/mo) got the nod for established sites with recurring spikes.
4. **WordPress you don't want to maintain.** GoDaddy's managed WordPress at $5.99 for the first term includes weekly backups, automatic core updates, a migration tool, and site security built in. Kinsta is the step up at $35/mo with a free static site option.
5. **Stores where downtime costs money.** TechRadar's eCommerce pick is ScalaHosting, whose cloud infrastructure adds scalability and redundancy. The same review cautions it's less beginner-friendly than builder-focused alternatives, so expect a more hands-on setup.
6. **Windows or ASP.NET apps.** GoDaddy sells Windows VPS plans with Plesk Obsidian; most of the shared-hosting market is Linux-only.
7. **Dev projects and self-hosted apps.** This is VPS territory. The mainstream reference point is InMotion at $17.99/mo for a discounted 8 vCPU plan. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much do web hosting services cost?**
For shared hosting, budget $2–$6/mo in year one at the major brands and roughly $5–$14/mo after renewal, based on the rates above. Managed WordPress starts at $35/mo (Kinsta). Mainstream VPS deals run about $18/mo, and budget VPS providers price by annual promotion.

**Is the cheapest plan enough for a new site?**
Usually, yes. GoDaddy's entry Economy plan — 25 GB NVMe, one website, free SSL for the first year, daily backups, a 30-day money-back guarantee — covers a typical blog or small business site with room to spare. The watch-outs are resource caps and inode limits, not the sticker price.

**What's the difference between a domain and web hosting?**
A domain is your address; hosting is the server it points to. You need both, and most hosts bundle the domain free for the first year — GoDaddy, Hostinger, and Bluehost all include one. GoDaddy happens to be the largest domain registrar globally, but you can register the domain and buy hosting from the same company or different ones.

**Can I switch hosts later?**
Yes, and migration support is common. Bluehost includes a free site transfer, Hosting.com offers free unlimited migrations, and GoDaddy's managed WordPress plans include a migration tool. Check before buying if you already have a live site.

**Do I need a VPS?**
Only if you need root access, custom server software, or guaranteed dedicated resources. A blog, portfolio, brochure site, or standard WordPress install doesn't. If you're building apps with AI coding tools like Claude or Cursor, HostingStep notes Hostinger's plans can host those directly, which is simpler than administering your own server.
